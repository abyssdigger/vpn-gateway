# EXP-085 result — full-tunnel aggressive escape guard

**Date:** 2026-11-22  
**Technical result:** **PASS**  
**Workflow run:** `45045697721`  
**Job:** `linux-host-managed-routing`

## Proven

The EXP-084 escape-guard pattern was widened to managed scope `0.0.0.0/0` and strengthened
with two explicit exemptions:

- HOST OUTPUT to a kernel-local destination remains allowed;
- FORWARD traffic entering HOST from the exact managed boundary remains allowed.

With correct aggressive routing and the guard active:

- host-originated full-tunnel traffic reached the remote target through the managed boundary;
- forwarded LAN full-tunnel traffic reached the remote target through the managed boundary;
- host-local delivery remained reachable;
- traffic arriving from the VPN-GATE side and leaving toward the ordinary LAN remained
  reachable.

After inserting an earlier foreign RPDB rule that selected ordinary host `main` routing:

- route lookup for managed outbound traffic changed to the ordinary uplink;
- local and forwarded managed outbound traffic were dropped by the guard;
- host-local delivery remained reachable;
- managed-boundary -> ordinary return traffic remained reachable.

After removing the foreign rule, managed outbound traffic recovered while the guard remained
installed.

## Disposition

**Selected evidence for E.004 lower escape-protection semantics.**

A production guard must support full-tunnel scope without interpreting `0.0.0.0/0` as
"drop every non-boundary packet". It must preserve at least:

1. host-local delivery;
2. traffic already handed from VPN-GATE through the managed boundary toward the ordinary
   network.

This closes the evidence gap identified before productionizing E.004, but does not itself
materialize E.004.
