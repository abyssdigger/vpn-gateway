## Proven

In disposable ORD/HOST/STACK/VPN1/VPN2 network namespaces, the experiment first established
working bidirectional routed connectivity across the ordinary-side managed boundary and
between the two VPN-side paths.

The candidate protective lower composition then applied:

1. verified `net.ipv4.ip_forward=0` inside STACK/Private network;
2. an nftables `netdev` table on the HOST side with IPv4 DROP chains on both ingress and
   egress of the exact ordinary-side managed-boundary interface.

With both barriers active:

- ordinary -> VPN payload path was blocked: **PASS**;
- VPN -> ordinary payload path was blocked: **PASS**;
- VPN -> VPN routed transit was blocked: **PASS**;
- the managed boundary interface remained present and UP: **PASS**;
- ordinary-host routing objects used by the test topology remained present: **PASS**.

After removing the host-boundary nft gate and explicitly restoring Private-network
forwarding:

- all three payload paths became reachable again: **PASS**.

The workflow also reran EXP-030 and the production forwarding-gate integration successfully
in the same job.

## Interpretation

This establishes a feasible Linux lower composition for the *traffic postcondition* of
`VPNG-U004-R5`: the required three traffic classes can be blocked without tearing down the
runtime topology.

It does not establish architecture closure for protective mode. Still required are:

- a public semantic enter/leave contract;
- component ownership of active protective-mode state;
- fresh observation and continuous maintenance of both lower barriers while the mode is
  active;
- transition ordering and failure semantics;
- safe exit semantics and revalidation before traffic is released;
- composed production-shaped acceptance evidence.

The current `ForwardingGate` therefore remains a reusable lower Enforcer rather than being
redefined as the public protective-mode owner. A separate host-boundary lower gate is also
required by the proven composition.
